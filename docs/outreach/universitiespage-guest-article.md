# MBBS in China 2027: a 10-point checklist before you pay any deposit

Thousands of students ask every year whether they can study medicine in China in English. Many can, but the difference between a safe choice and an expensive mistake is usually decided before the first payment. Use these ten checks. Every fact below comes from published notices and fee structures, and each point says what to ask for in writing.

## 1. Is the university on the Ministry of Education list?

China's Ministry of Education (MOE) publishes the list of universities approved to teach MBBS in English to international students. The notice of 1 June 2026 covers the 2026-2027 academic year: 43 universities and 2,790 seats. Ask the university for its position and seat number on that list. For example, Xinjiang Medical University (XJMU) is number 8, with 60 seats. A new list for 2027-2028 has not been published yet, so do not trust any page that claims one.

## 2. What happens if the university is not on the list?

Under the notice, a university that is not listed may admit international students to clinical medicine taught in Chinese only, and may not issue an English-language MBBS certificate. Teaching "bilingually" is not allowed as a workaround. If an agent says an unlisted university offers English MBBS, ask for the MOE paragraph that says so.

## 3. Get the fees in yuan and in writing

Universities in China charge in Chinese yuan. Ask what the first-year package covers and what each later year costs. At XJMU, the 2027 fees are unchanged from 2026: a first-year package of ¥49,950, which includes application processing, registration, tuition, hostel accommodation, the student visa, health insurance and medical checks, the JW202 visa letter and airport pickup, then ¥35,950 a year from year two. Convert to your own currency only for planning: the rate changes every day and banks add a margin.

## 4. Check the entry requirements against your own marks

Requirements differ between universities. XJMU asks for a senior school certificate (HSSC or FSc pre-medical, A-Level or equivalent) with at least 60% in each of physics, chemistry and biology, an age of 18 to 35, good health and a valid passport. You do not need Chinese to apply. Ask for these conditions in writing and compare them with your certificate before you pay an application fee.

## 5. Read your own medical council's current rules

A Chinese degree is only the first step. Your home council decides what a foreign medical graduate must pass or hold before registration, and it can change its rules. Read the council's current published rules yourself, and keep a copy with the date. No university, agent or website can promise that you will be registered.

## 6. Understand the visa

A degree student who stays more than 180 days applies for an X1 visa with the admission letter and the JW201 or JW202 form, then applies for a residence permit after arriving. Read the visa page of the Chinese embassy or consulate for your own country, because document lists differ. Nobody can guarantee a visa; the consular officer decides.

## 7. Ask about the Chinese language requirement

The medical course is taught in English, but patients in hospitals speak Chinese, and Chinese lessons are part of the timetable in the early years. At XJMU, HSK level 4 is required by graduation. Ask how many hours of Chinese are taught and what the university requires.

## 8. Know the intakes and the deadlines

XJMU has a March/Spring intake and a September/Fall intake. The application fee is ¥1,000, and a ¥2,000 seat deposit follows the Pre-Admission Letter. Apply early: seats are limited, and documents such as the police certificate and the medical form take weeks to collect.

## 9. Pay safely

Pay only after you have the fee structure in writing, and only to the account the university names on its own official channels. Confirm the account details with the university directly, not only with an agent. Keep every receipt. Do not pay for a "guaranteed" scholarship, visa or seat.

## 10. Verify the admission letter and the person

Ask the university to confirm that your letter and your agent are genuine, using the contact details on its official website. A real university will answer.
